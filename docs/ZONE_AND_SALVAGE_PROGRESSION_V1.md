# Zone & Salvage Progression V1

Status: **DESIGN BASELINE FOR REVIEW**

This document defines the progression of active scavenging maps and how old maps convert into passive worker content after player mastery.

## Zone philosophy

A new zone must feel richer or mechanically different, not merely tougher.

Each frontier zone introduces at least one of:
- a new target family;
- a new raw resource;
- a new component family;
- a new logistics pattern;
- a new heavy-target interaction;
- a new contract type.

Old zones remain playable forever and intentionally become easy power-fantasy content as the player's tool/skills improve.

## Zone mastery

Each zone owns a visible mastery meter.

Mastery progress is earned only by the player through active play:
- dismantling targets;
- returning haul from that zone;
- discovering its signature target/component.

The final mastery threshold also requires the zone's signature salvage to have been recovered at least once.

Target feel:
- early zone: ~2–4 active runs to mastery once progression reaches it;
- mid zone: ~3–6 active runs;
- late zone: ~4–8 active runs.

Exact numbers come later from simulation.

Once mastered:
- worker assignment becomes legal;
- ordinary resource/component output can become passive;
- signature component becomes eligible for low-rate passive return only after first manual discovery;
- player can still visit manually at any time.

## Zone ladder

### Z1 — Old Junkyard
Target phase: 0–4h.

Identity:
- dense miscellaneous household junk;
- fast destruction rhythm;
- teaches cart capacity and keep-vs-sell.

Typical targets:
- cans / sheet scrap / pipes;
- cable bundles;
- fans;
- small appliances;
- bicycles;
- washer / fridge class appliances.

Primary raw resources:
- ferrous scrap;
- copper;
- plastic.

Primary components:
- bearings;
- electric motors;
- hydraulic units;
- electronic modules.

Signature discovery:
- first hydraulic unit / large appliance salvage.

Long-term role:
- best cheap ferrous/plastic source;
- first zone to automate;
- spectacular late-game return because nearly everything breaks instantly.

### Z2 — Garage Cooperative / Backyards
Target phase: ~4–8h.

Identity:
- denser mechanical/electrical junk;
- better component hunting than Z1;
- more compact routes and more medium targets.

Typical targets:
- power tools;
- pumps;
- compressors;
- lawn/garage equipment;
- moped/bike assemblies;
- workshop fans / motors.

Resource shift:
- less cheap plastic clutter;
- more copper;
- more motors/electronics.

New component families:
- **Pump**;
- **Compressor**.

Signature discovery:
- first intact pump or compressor.

Unlock intent:
- equipped early garage;
- first press running;
- meaningful tool/cart progression.

Long-term role:
- passive motors, copper, pumps and compressors for mid-game recipes.

### Z3 — Municipal Dump / Appliance Yard
Target phase: ~8–15h.

Identity:
- much larger appliances and mixed piles;
- van changes run structure by allowing repeated cart unloads;
- better bulk yield.

Typical targets:
- refrigerators / freezers;
- washers / dryers;
- HVAC units;
- ovens;
- vending/commercial appliances;
- stacked mixed-scrap piles.

New raw resource:
- **Aluminium scrap** begins here at a moderate rate.

Primary valuable components:
- compressors;
- larger motors;
- electronic modules;
- pumps.

Signature discovery:
- high-quality compressor / HVAC assembly.

Unlock intent:
- van acquired or immediately adjacent to acquisition;
- cart remains used between target field and van.

Long-term role:
- bulk aluminium / copper / compressor supply for hangar-era equipment.

### Z4 — Auto Salvage Yard
Target phase: ~15–30h.

Identity:
- recognizable vehicle assemblies;
- fewer tiny objects, more medium/heavy subassemblies;
- introduces the first deliberate intact-vs-smash choices on premium parts.

Typical targets:
- wheels / hubs;
- starters / alternators;
- doors / body panels;
- radiators;
- seats / interior assemblies only as low-priority clutter;
- engines/transmissions as heavy nodes;
- stripped vehicle shells.

Resource mix:
- ferrous scrap;
- aluminium;
- copper;
- reduced plastic relevance.

New component families:
- **Gearbox**;
- **Generator / Alternator unit**.

Signature discovery:
- first intact gearbox.

Optional premium-target decision:
- careful extraction preserves a valuable assembly;
- brute-force destruction yields more bulk material faster.

No precision minigame is required; this is a route/target-mode decision.

Long-term role:
- passive aluminium, gearboxes and generators;
- major input for transport upgrades.

### Z5 — Industrial Zone
Target phase: ~28–50h / Hangar era.

Identity:
- physically larger machines;
- lower object count but much richer objects;
- powered tools begin to matter;
- multi-stage target silhouettes appear.

Typical targets:
- industrial motors;
- pumps;
- hydraulic power packs;
- electrical cabinets;
- small machine tools;
- conveyors;
- compressors;
- steel frames.

New raw resource:
- **Alloy scrap** at low/moderate rate.

New component family:
- **Industrial Controller**.

Signature discovery:
- first industrial controller from a cabinet/machine core.

Heavy-target pattern:
- outer structure -> subsystem -> valuable core;
- stages are broken by the normal automatic tool system as the player moves/prioritizes.

Long-term role:
- critical passive source for alloy, heavy motors, controllers and hydraulics.

### Z6 — Abandoned Factory
Target phase: ~45–80h / Salvage Yard era.

Identity:
- full production-equipment remains;
- strong concentration of advanced parts;
- new targets often require truck/winch/tool-class prerequisites.

Typical targets:
- lathes / mills;
- industrial presses;
- large electrical cabinets;
- pump skids;
- production-line sections;
- cooling units;
- robot/actuator assemblies where appropriate to style.

New component family:
- **Heavy Hydraulic Cylinder**.

Resource mix:
- alloy scrap;
- steel/ferrous bulk;
- copper;
- aluminium.

Signature discovery:
- heavy hydraulic cylinder or high-grade industrial controller.

Long-term role:
- passive advanced-component backbone for industrial workshop construction.

### Z7 — Heavy Depot / Construction Equipment Yard
Target phase: ~75–120h.

Identity:
- fewer but very heavy targets;
- recovery rig / truck positioning matters;
- parts are valuable enough that targeted extraction becomes a real choice.

Typical targets:
- forklifts;
- generators;
- compressors;
- construction attachments;
- hydraulic machinery;
- large diesel/electric equipment assemblies;
- heavy frames.

No new common raw-resource family is required here; depth comes from quality and quantity.

High-value outputs:
- heavy hydraulic cylinders;
- generators;
- gearboxes;
- industrial controllers;
- alloy scrap.

Signature discovery:
- heavy power/recovery module used by late transport or factory recipes.

Long-term role:
- passive premium mechanical components.

### Z8 — Port / Container Terminal
Target phase: 120h+ / Processing Plant.

Identity:
- true late-game frontier;
- very large and valuable industrial objects;
- container/yard machinery creates multi-stage salvage events;
- highest active-value density.

Typical targets:
- forklifts / reach-stackers;
- container refrigeration units;
- generators;
- cranes/hoists as partial recoverable systems rather than entire realistic cranes;
- heavy motors;
- industrial power cabinets;
- damaged cargo machinery.

Outputs:
- all advanced metals;
- high-tier components;
- premium contract salvage;
- unique late recipes/signature modules.

Signature discovery:
- premium industrial power/control assembly.

Long-term role:
- not intended to become irrelevant even after automation because premium/manual extraction remains better than passive yield.

## Map size and generation

Do not grow maps into empty open worlds.

Each zone should remain a compact arcade map with:
- high target density;
- recognizable route loops;
- 2–4 layout variants / seeded arrangements;
- different clutter/target composition per visit;
- clear return/unload points for van/truck-era logistics.

A bigger transport tier changes the **length of an expedition**, not necessarily the literal screen dimensions by 10x.

## Worker production from zones

Worker production is an abstraction of repeated known runs.

Baseline model:

`zone knowledge × worker efficiency × assigned transport × elapsed time × storage availability`

Workers should return:
- common raw resources reliably;
- discovered common components occasionally;
- signature/rare components at a much lower rate than focused active play.

A worker route stops or slows when:
- its vehicle buffer is full;
- base storage is full;
- required transport tier is missing;
- the worker/vehicle is reassigned.

Offline report must say why throughput stopped.

## Targeted farming rule

Even after automation, active player farming is best when the player specifically needs a component.

Example:
- automated Z4 produces some gearboxes over time;
- personally entering Z4 and deliberately hunting transmission targets gives a much higher gearbox/hour rate.

This keeps old zones economically relevant without forcing grind.

## Resource introduction rule

Do not introduce a new resource merely for realism.

A new raw material/component is allowed only when all three exist together:
1. recognizable source targets;
2. at least one desirable recipe immediately or very soon;
3. a clear reason the existing resource set cannot serve that role.
